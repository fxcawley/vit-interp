## A. The two 2026 results this direction sits between

**Joseph et al., Interpreting Physics in Video World Models (arXiv:2602.07050, Feb 2026)** [verified 2026-10-13, HTML read]. First interpretability study of V-JEPA 2 (L, H, G) and VideoMAE-v2-G internals. Findings used here: (1) a "Physics Emergence Zone" at about one-third depth where possible-vs-impossible and motion-direction information become linearly accessible, peaking mid-depth and decaying toward the output; (2) direction-selective MLP units at the end of the zone tile 360 degrees with smooth sinusoidal tuning and form a unit-circle population code (their Fig. 4); (3) the orthogonal-probe sequence shows a sawtooth "consistent with paired (e.g., sine-cosine) feature encodings"; (4) steering requires coordinated intervention over many orthogonal probe directions (< 0.5 degree error with all probes at layer 8 vs > 80 degrees for one); (5) they note direction "becomes spatially redundant across patches post-Physics Emergence Zone" (App. C.5); (6) their direction stimuli are Kubric videos of one sphere, 8 directions x 7 speeds x 7 start positions = 392 clips, 16 frames at 256x256; direction probes are circular regressions onto (sin, cos). Interpretation offered: support for distributed, task-specific computation over compact latent state (Sec. 8.1), with a neuroscience parallel to MT direction tuning (Sec. 8.2). Author list [verified from arXiv BibTeX 2026-10-13]: Sonia Joseph, Quentin Garrido, Randall Balestriero, Matthew Kowal, Thomas Fel, Shahab Bakhtiari, Blake Richards, Mike Rabbat. So the LeJEPA theory group (Balestriero), the Archetypal-SAE author (Fel) and a neuroscience lab (Richards) are already on the interpretability side of this question. A third-party reproduction exists (github.com/hafeezkhan909/vjepa-physics-interp) [verified via search 2026-10-13], so the probing pipeline is reproducible.

What they did not do: decompose the direction subspace into harmonics; separate harmonic multiplicity, spatial copies and speed interactions in the dimensionality count; use continuous directions (8 directions alias harmonics above k = 3); relate the geometry to the training objective.

**Klindt, LeCun, Balestriero, When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model? (arXiv:2605.26379, May 2026)** [verified 2026-10-13, HTML read]. Proves LeJEPA (alignment + Gaussian regularization) is linearly identifiable iff the latents are Gaussian, for stationary additive-noise transitions, via a spectral decomposition: for Gaussian worlds the transition operator's eigenfunctions are Hermite polynomials with eigenvalue rho^k (Mehler), alignment penalizes each degree of nonlinearity, so the optimum is linear. Appendix F: for non-Gaussian latents the transition operator still has a spectral decomposition with ordered eigenvalues, "the first eigenfunction is monotonic but generally nonlinear, which is why monotonic (not linear) identifiability holds in the general case", with explicit connections to slow feature analysis and harmonic analysis. Experiments: 2-D mixing (shear, RealNVP), a latent-distribution sweep over the generalized normal family (recovery peaks at Gaussian), and Reacher joint-angle trajectories, which show R^2 <= 0.5 and are attributed to "non-Gaussian marginals, anisotropic rho, joint-limit wrapping" together.

What they did not do: treat periodic latents as their own case (the "monotone first eigenfunction" argument presumes an interval; on a circle the first nontrivial eigenspace is two-dimensional); measure code geometry rather than R^2; vary the regularizer weight or the width as experimental knobs.

## B. The September 2026 paper that took the theory plank

**Nicollier, Meinhardt-Llopis, Pic, Muse, Facciolo, Beyond Gaussian Worlds: Latent Geometry Matters for JEPAs (arXiv:2609.21656, 18 Sep 2026)** [verified 2026-10-13, PDF read]. Extends Klindt et al. to latents on embedded Riemannian manifolds with intrinsic diffusions. Results: with a spherical target matched to spherical latents, every optimum recovers the latent up to an orthogonal map; Gaussian uniqueness is not universal; an approximate-recovery bound tighter for the sphere than for the Gaussian; geometry-aware heat-kernel MMD regularizers for sphere, torus, Clifford torus. Experiments are pixel-based at 128x128: a Cartesian-robot renderer (Gaussian OU, rho = 0.95), the DM Control reacher-hard two-link arm with joint angles uniform on T^2 evolved by a torus diffusion, and the Stanford bunny under spherical Brownian camera motion; encoder is an ImageNet-pretrained EdgeNeXt-XX-Small plus linear projection; 50 epochs. Key numbers: Gaussian target on the torus world gives R^2(Z to Y) = 0.986 but R^2(Y to Z) = 0.493; matched torus target reaches R^2(Y to Z) = 0.996 but only in 5 of 10 seeds ("optimization is sensitive to initialization"); a "Gaussian rank bottleneck" is mentioned when comparing d vs d+2 coordinates. They cite Wiskott and Sejnowski (SFA) and Coifman's diffusion-map eigenfunction work. Code promised on acceptance. The paper discloses generative-AI assistance in writing and code.

What they did not do: characterize the distorted code (no harmonic analysis; the word does not occur in the text I extracted); vary lambda as an experimental variable (lambda was selected per world-target pair on seed 0); compare with an EMA-teacher recipe that has no distributional target; analyze released models; connect to the interpretability finding of Joseph et al.

Related from the same group: SPHERE-JEPA (arXiv:2605.26900) and Expanding SPHERE-JEPA (arXiv:2606.17603) [titles verified 2026-10-13], the spherical-target JEPAs whose existence makes "which target for which latent" a live design question.

## C. Other 2026 JEPA theory

- Cui et al., A Generalization Theory for JEPA-Based World Models (arXiv:2606.27014) [verified 2026-10-13, abstract]: JEPA pretraining as conditional spectral graph learning; the objective is a low-rank factorization of an action-conditioned co-occurrence matrix; trade-off governed by latent dimensionality. Supports the spectral framing used here.
- On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models (arXiv:2607.22430) [verified 2026-10-13, abstract]: joint identifiability of representation and transition for action-conditioned LeJEPA with Gaussian latents; "spectral separation property".
- Spectral-Target Physical Latent Structuring for JEPA-Style World Models (arXiv:2609.04264) [verified 2026-10-13, abstract]: identifies "physical representation laziness" in LeWorldModel (latents do not collapse but fail to represent key physical properties) and adds a supervised "Fourier auxiliary head". Relevant twice: laziness is what H3 predicts at small lambda, and their fix presupposes that a Fourier basis is the right structure for physical variables.
- Nelson et al., Statistical and structural identifiability in representation learning (arXiv:2603.11970, ICLR 2026) [verified via search 2026-10-13]: separates consistency across runs (statistical) from alignment with ground truth (structural). Our cross-seed prediction (harmonic subspaces align, coordinates do not) is a concrete instance of their distinction.
- LeJEPA (arXiv:2511.08544) [verified 2026-10-13, abstract] and LeWorldModel (arXiv:2603.19312) [verified 2026-10-13, HTML read]: SIGReg matches embeddings to N(0, I) through Epps-Pulley tests on random 1-D projections; LeWM = next-embedding prediction + lambda SIGReg, encoder maps frames to a compact low-dimensional latent, trains in hours on one GPU; official code at github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm [verified via search]; a companion "stable-worldmodel-v1" evaluation suite is cited.
- Littwin et al., How JEPA Avoids Noisy Features (arXiv:2407.03475) [title verified]: deep linear analysis of JEPA preferring high-influence features.
- V-JEPA 2 (arXiv:2506.09985), V-JEPA 2.1 (arXiv:2603.14482), Garrido et al. intuitive physics (arXiv:2502.11831) [titles verified]: the models and the behavioral "world model" evidence that Joseph et al. interpret.

## D. The classical theory that makes the predictions (this is the mechanism)

- Wiskott and Sejnowski, Slow Feature Analysis (Neural Computation 2002) [to verify details]: extract the slowest-varying decorrelated unit-variance functions of the input; the solutions are the leading eigenfunctions of a temporal-difference operator. Wiskott (2003), "Slow feature analysis: a theoretical analysis of optimal free responses" (Neural Computation) [to verify]: for a periodic latent the optimal responses are harmonics ordered by frequency.
- Franzius, Sprekeler, Wiskott, Slowness and Sparseness Lead to Place, Head-Direction, and Spatial-View Cells (PLoS Comput Biol 2007) [verified via search 2026-10-13]: SFA on simulated visual input from a moving agent yields head-direction units with sinusoidal tuning; the harmonics of orientation are among the slow features.
- Sprekeler, On the relation of slow feature analysis and Laplacian eigenmaps (Neural Computation 2011) [to verify]: SFA solutions are Laplacian eigenfunctions of the data manifold under the dynamics.
- Balestriero and LeCun, Contrastive and Non-Contrastive SSL Recover Global and Local Spectral Embedding Methods (arXiv:2205.11508) [title verified]; HaoChen et al., spectral contrastive loss (arXiv:2106.04156) [title verified]: modern SSL objectives are spectral embeddings of the positive-pair graph. Together with Cui et al. and Klindt's Appendix F, this is the warrant for "a JEPA fills its width with the top eigenfunctions of the transition operator".
- Coifman and Lafon, diffusion maps and eigenfunctions of Fokker-Planck operators (NeurIPS 2005) [to verify]: cited by Nicollier; the mathematical object is the same.

Prior art summary for the paper: the prediction "slowness learners represent a circular variable as Fourier harmonics ordered by frequency" is twenty years old. Our contribution is not that prediction; it is testing it in JEPAs with distributional regularizers, adding the regularizer-width mechanism, and using it to reread 2026 results.

## E. Topology and symmetry in representation learning (why two dimensions are necessary, and what "identifiable up to phase" means)

- Batson, Haaf, Kahn, Roberts, Topological Obstructions to Autoencoding (arXiv:2102.08380) [title verified]: a circle cannot be embedded continuously in R^1; low-dimensional bottlenecks on circular factors produce tearing or distributed codes.
- Falorsi et al., Explorations in Homeomorphic VAEs (arXiv:1807.04689); Davidson et al., Hyperspherical VAEs (arXiv:1804.00891) [titles verified]: Euclidean latent priors mismatch S^1 and SO(3); manifold-valued latents fix it. The VAE-era precedent for Nicollier's "match the target to the geometry".
- Higgins et al., Towards a Definition of Disentangled Representations (arXiv:1812.02230); Quessard et al., Learning Group Structure and Disentangled Representations of Dynamical Environments (arXiv:2002.06991); Marchetti et al., Equivariant Representation Learning via Class-Pose Decomposition (arXiv:2207.03116); Keurti et al., Homomorphism Autoencoder (arXiv:2207.12067); Pfau et al., Disentangling by Subspace Diffusion (arXiv:2006.12982) [titles verified]: symmetry-based disentanglement represents a rotation group action as a rotation in a 2-D subspace, i.e., (cos, sin) up to phase. Our d = 2 prediction is their equivariant code; our d > 2 prediction is the tower of irreducible representations (harmonics) of SO(2).

## F. Identifiability background

- Hyvarinen and Morioka, TCL (arXiv:1605.06336); Khemakhem et al., iVAE (arXiv:1907.04809); Klindt et al., SlowVAE (arXiv:2007.10930); Roeder, Metz, Kingma, On Linear Identifiability of Learned Representations (arXiv:2007.00810); Zimmermann et al., Contrastive Learning Inverts the Data Generating Process (arXiv:2102.08850) [titles verified]: identifiability up to linear or orthogonal maps for discriminative/contrastive objectives under stated latent distributions (uniform on the hypersphere in Zimmermann et al., which is the spherical case Nicollier revisits).
- Wang and Isola, alignment and uniformity on the hypersphere (arXiv:2005.10242); VICReg (arXiv:2105.04906) [titles verified]: the two regularizer families (uniform-on-sphere vs variance-covariance) whose behavior on circular latents H3b distinguishes from SIGReg.

## G. Neuroscience of periodic variables (the strongest external prior that harmonic ring codes are the norm, not a failure)

- Cueva et al., Emergence of functional and structural properties of the head direction system by optimization of recurrent neural networks (arXiv:1912.10189, ICLR 2020) [title verified]: RNNs trained to integrate heading develop ring attractors with sinusoidal tuning.
- Dorrell et al., Actionable Neural Representations: Grid Cells from Minimal Constraints (arXiv:2209.15563, ICLR 2023) [title verified]: representations of a periodic/position variable under actionability, non-negativity and capacity constraints are Fourier-like; the number of modules relates to the constraints. Methodologically the closest analogue to "constraints set the harmonic content".
- Schaeffer et al., self-supervised learning of representations for space generates multi-modular grid cells (NeurIPS 2023) [to verify ID]; Gardner et al., toroidal topology of grid-cell population activity (Nature 2022) [to verify]: population codes for periodic variables live on rings and tori in the brain.
- Joseph et al. themselves cite MT direction tuning (Albright 1984) as the parallel.

## H2. Framing sources added 2026-10-13 (flavor only; see `03-positioning.md` 4b)

- Pres, Li, Ruis, Guo, Hu, Damani, Puri, Lubana, Andreas. Position: It's Time to Optimize LLMs for Self-Consistency (arXiv:2608.05188, ICML 2026 Position track) [verified 2026-10-13, HTML read]. Relations between a model's outputs across related inputs as the primary object; instance-level invariance and equivariance families with a consistency function phi; meta-level self-description consistency; optimization as hard constraint or lambda-weighted regularizer. Used here only for the formal observation that JEPA alignment is a consistency objective and that harmonic structure is equivariance consistency. Companion: Self-CTRL (arXiv:2606.18327) [verified abstract], LLM-side, not used.
- Cammarata, Neural Networks Are Already Clean (Goodfire essay, already-clean.goodfire.com, 15 Oct 2026) [read via LessWrong linkpost and secondary summaries only; the host is blocked by KLA's proxy]. Claims: most neurons in large models are legible (~90% of Qwen3-32B neuron poles, preliminary, threshold-dependent); early polysemanticity came from fixable architecture and tooling issues rather than superposition; retrained InceptionV1 last layer near-fully interpretable; checkpoint trajectories as the discriminating test. Contested (Nanda: superposition still real; skeptical of vision-to-LLM transfer). Motivates E1 only.
- Huang, Zhang, et al. Long-WAM: Scaling the Context of World-Action Models (NVIDIA/MIT/HKU/UCSD, 2026; project page nvlabs.github.io/LongLive/Long-WAM) [verified 2026-10-13, project page]. Real-time control needs visual history to infer motion and task progress; longer history helps only with autoregressive video pretraining (bidirectional init shows no gain from 2.4 s to 19.2 s). Motivates the observability knob E2 and the limitation paragraph.
- Social graph worth knowing: Lubana is on the self-consistency position paper and on Rabbit Hull; Rabbit Hull also lists Fel, Balestriero, Joseph and Kowal; Joseph et al. lists Balestriero, Fel and Richards; Lubana and Cammarata are at Goodfire.
